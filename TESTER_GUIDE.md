# RESEARCH PASS — QUICK TESTER GUIDE

Thanks for helping test the Universal Research Pass.

You do **not** need to be good at prompting, research, or AI.

In fact, please use it naturally.

---

# What This Is

The Research Pass is a system designed to make an AI **investigate a question instead of just answering it**.

It is supposed to:

- challenge assumptions
- look for competing explanations
- verify important claims
- find contradictions
- use stronger evidence
- explain uncertainty
- stop when it has enough information to give a useful answer

Your job is to find out whether it actually does that.

---

# Step 1 — Pick Something You Really Want to Know

Choose a real question.

Good examples:

- something you are curious about
- a decision you need to make
- something you already have a strong opinion about
- an obscure topic
- a claim you are unsure is true
- a complicated “why does this happen?” question

Do not worry about making the question sound smart.

Messy questions are useful.

---

# Step 2 — Run the Normal Version

Open a fresh AI chat.

Ask your question normally.

Example:

> Why do people remember embarrassing moments for so long?

Save or keep that answer.

---

# Step 3 — Run the Research Pass Version

Open another fresh chat using the same AI model if possible.

Give the AI the current canonical `RESEARCH_PASS.md`.

Then type:

> **Research Pass this: [your exact same question]**

Example:

> Research Pass this: Why do people remember embarrassing moments for so long?

Let it work.

Do not try to rescue it or tell it how to research unless it genuinely needs information from you.

---

# Step 4 — Compare Them

Ask yourself:

- Which answer taught me more?
- Which one used better evidence?
- Did the Research Pass challenge assumptions?
- Did it find explanations the normal answer missed?
- Did it handle uncertainty better?
- Did it research unnecessary things?
- Was it too long?
- Did it misunderstand what I wanted?
- Did it make anything up?
- Did it reach a better conclusion?
- Would I actually prefer using it again?

---

# Step 5 — Tell Us What Happened

The most useful feedback is specific.

Good:

> It found two competing explanations the normal answer missed, but spent way too much time researching a simple part of the question.

Very good:

> It confidently accepted a false premise in my question. I repeated the test twice and it did the same thing.

Also valuable:

> Honestly, the normal answer was better.

Failure reports are not bad for the project.

**Failures are exactly what we are looking for.**

---

# Please Do Not Modify It Yet

For your first test:

> **Use the canonical Research Pass exactly as written.**

After you test it once, you are welcome to modify, shorten, rewrite, or specialize it.

But we need the original result first so we have something to compare against.

---

# If You Want to Help More

Try three questions:

1. something you personally care about
2. something complicated or obscure
3. something where you already think you know the answer

Different kinds of questions help us find different weaknesses.

---

# Final Rule

Do not try to prove that the Research Pass is good.

Try to discover:

> **Where does it work, where does it fail, and why?**
